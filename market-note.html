Market note / May 2026

Where should UK schools expand next?

Observations
  • Scale & growth: international schools now number 15,075 worldwide, with 7.6m students and $69.3bn of annual fee income.
  • British brands: British private schools operated 151 satellite campuses in 2024-25, concentrated in China/Hong Kong, wider Asia and the Middle East.
  • Location shift: the historic China/UAE/Thailand playbook is becoming more selective; non-UAE GCC markets, Hong Kong and certain US-led partnership models now deserve closer review.

Global demand continues to compound.

The market is no longer niche. It is a large, mature K-12 system where the best locations are increasingly defined by partner quality and regulatory fit, not only demand.

The installed base explains where British brands have gone. It does not fully answer where the next campus or partnership should be.

Preferred locations now need a sharper filter.

Prioritise Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Oman

Government-backed demand, fewer premium British competitors and stronger economics where the partner is credible.

Selective UAE, Hong Kong, Singapore

Strong demand and deep talent pools, but high competition, cost and brand-noise require careful positioning.

Watch US, Vietnam, Indonesia

Attractive for partnerships, pathways or selective campus models, but not always a straightforward franchise play.

Caution Mainland China, Thailand

Large installed bases and proven demand, but saturation, regulation and differentiation risks have increased.

Middle East

The Gulf has not become less important. It has become less forgiving.

01

Demand remains, but volatility is now a board issue.

S&P expects GCC growth to remain a driver of MENA activity, while unresolved conflict pathways keep the region exposed to geopolitical risk.

02

Dubai is large, attractive and crowded.

Dubai recorded 387,441 private-school students in 2024-25, with UK curriculum the largest segment. More supply is still being added.

03

Non-UAE GCC economics may be stronger.

Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Oman can offer a better combination of policy support, parent demand and lower premium-brand density.

04

Execution risk has moved up the agenda.

Energy, shipping, aviation and insurance disruption make partner bankability and operating continuity more important than headline market size.

Implications

What this means for UK schools.

Do not let the first investor approach define the market strategy.

Separate demand from deliverability: regulation, land, staffing and governance can change the answer.

Test partner quality before negotiating economics; the wrong partner can create long-term brand risk.

Use a short board review to compare franchise, joint venture and operator-led routes before committing.
